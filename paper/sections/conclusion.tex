\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented \styleshield{}, a flow-matching-based diagnostic framework that exposes the fragility of AIGC detectors through continuous, controllable style transfer.
By combining a pretrained DiT backbone with zero-initialized cross-attention adapters conditioned on frozen Qwen-7B representations, \styleshield{} achieves $\geq$94.6\% evasion on the training detector and $\geq$99\% on three unseen detectors, while preserving 0.928 semantic similarity.
RateAudit further demonstrates that document-level detection verdicts can be shifted to arbitrary pre-specified values---a result that, in our view, should disqualify percentage-based scores as evidence in any consequential decision.

\paragraph{Broader Impact.}
We do not build \styleshield{} to help anyone cheat.
We build it because the AIGC detection industry is already making high-stakes judgments---academic penalties, hiring filters, content removal---on the basis of classifiers whose robustness has never been independently audited.
Our results provide that audit: a single lightweight model, trained with standard hardware and publicly available LLMs, is sufficient to render four distinct detectors unreliable.

The deeper issue is not that detectors can be fooled---any classifier can---but that the premise itself is flawed.
The AI--human stylistic boundary is dissolving as LLMs train on human corpora and humans write with AI assistance; the statistical features detectors exploit are transient artifacts, not stable category markers.
Deploying such tools for academic sanctions or employment decisions without rigorous, independent validation is irresponsible.
We urge the community to move beyond origin-based detection toward evaluation of what actually matters: whether text demonstrates understanding, critical thinking, and originality---qualities that are assessable regardless of how the text was produced.

Code and model weights will be released upon acceptance.
